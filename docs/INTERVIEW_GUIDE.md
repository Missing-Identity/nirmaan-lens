# Domain Interview Guide

## Goal

Validate that regulation lookup and preliminary proposal checking are recurring, costly workflows before fixing the MVP scope or building the corpus around assumptions.

Target 10–15 interviews:

- 4–5 architects or civil engineers
- 2–3 building-permission consultants
- 2–3 small builders
- 2–3 recent homeowners or applicants

## Opening

Explain that the interview concerns how people currently research development rules. Do not pitch the product until the current workflow has been described.

Do not collect client names, property addresses, survey numbers, unpublished plans, or confidential documents in public project notes.

## Questions

### Recent real example

1. Tell me about the last proposal where you had to verify a building rule.
2. What facts did you need before you could start?
3. Which websites, PDFs, colleagues, or consultants did you use?
4. Which part took the most time?
5. Did you encounter conflicting or amended rules?
6. How did you decide which source was current and applicable?
7. What was the consequence if the interpretation was wrong?

### Frequency and cost

8. How often does this happen?
9. Roughly how much time does one preliminary check take?
10. Which questions are routine, and which always require senior review?
11. When do you stop researching and ask a specialist?

### Evidence and trust

12. What would you need to see before trusting a software answer?
13. Are exact pages, highlighted clauses, or amendment history important?
14. When should the system refuse to answer?
15. Would a conditional answer be useful when one plot fact is missing?

### Product test

After the workflow questions, describe the four product modes briefly.

16. Which mode would you use first?
17. What is missing from the proposed input form?
18. What output would you give to a client or senior reviewer?
19. What would make this unsafe or unusable?
20. Would you test an early prototype using de-identified scenarios?

## Artifacts to collect

- De-identified question wording
- Required input fields
- Source families used
- Time spent and escalation point
- Failure consequences
- Trust and abstention requirements
- Willingness to test

## Decision gates

Proceed to implementation when:

- At least five professionals confirm a recurring workflow.
- At least 100 de-identified questions are collected.
- The top two or three rule families cover a coherent MVP.
- Authoritative sources are publicly accessible without bypassing controls.
- At least five professionals agree to review test cases or try the prototype.

Narrow or change the project if users primarily need title verification, full permit filing, or private consultant judgment rather than repeatable rule research.
